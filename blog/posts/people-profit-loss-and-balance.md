David Maister said the following more than 30 years ago and, as in most matters, he's both right and more right than ever: 

"The assets employed in the business are ... Staff, whose salaries are comparable to assets financed by debt, at a fixed interest rate."

### What does that mean? 

First let's set aside the ugly connotation that people can be reduced to numbers, to 'things.' We're talking about people being assets because they change over time, for better or worse, for both themselves and their employer. It means the people in the company need to deliver on the investment the company makes in them. And it means — sorry, owner-operators — that it's management's responsibility to improve the value of the asset over time. Put differently, it is the employee's responsibility to add value in the short-term, and the company's responsibility to add value in the long-term. 

If management sees employees only as a profit and loss statement ('how much can/did we extract?'), they will find themselves frustrated in the balance sheet ('this employee is getting worse, not better'). Short-term thinking with no consideration for the future lands you in the Bad Place: 

- Frustrated management, disappointed they have to constantly step in to heroically save the day; wishing their juniors could make better use of common sense.
- Confused juniors, given too little information to improve their pattern matching in live exercises, with too much pressure to deliver results that have no 'definition of done.'

If that sounds familiar, what's the alternative?
